# Privacy-Principled Member

## Role

A group member whose motivation is primarily values-driven. They want sovereignty because it aligns with who they want to be, not because they have calculated a specific risk exposure.

## Characteristics

- Stance categories: topological sovereignty ("content in my perimeter stays in my perimeter"), trust disposition, or consent obligations for third-party data held in trust.
- May have modest direct risk exposure but strong willingness-to-pay on principle.
- Likely handles third-party content — friends' disclosures, community work, personal health data — where consent for commercial AI processing was never given.
- Less technical than Cristos. Needs the SPA to explain ZDR vs. DPA, threat models, and capability tradeoffs without jargon.
- Compromise tolerance is low — "any subset I can protect is worth protecting."

## Needs from the SPA

- Calibration prompts that help anchor willingness-to-pay to real spending patterns (streaming, email, donations) rather than abstract principle.
- Clear explanation of what local hardware actually prevents vs. what it doesn't (supply-chain compromise affects both equally).
- Capability evaluation with curated samples matching their actual sovereignty-gated content categories (household PII, health, intimate content).
- Assurance that their values-based contribution is treated as legitimate justification, not dismissed as irrational.

## Red flags to watch

- Principle WTP inflated by social pressure or desire to support Cristos.
- Compromise tolerance set artificially low without considering what partial sovereignty actually means.
